## Phase 2.8 — Protection Scheduler and Cancellation Races

Phase 2.8 makes protection-window expiry entirely server-authoritative. Its central guarantee is:

> When customer Undo and automatic expiry compete, exactly one transition wins: `PROTECTED → CANCELLED` or `PROTECTED → RELEASED`.

The database remains the source of truth. Browser timers, scheduler memory, duplicate executions, application restarts, and multiple backend instances cannot independently determine the outcome.

## Critical invariants throughout Phase 2.8

- Only transactions still in `PROTECTED` may be automatically released.
- Only MEDIUM and HIGH timed routes are scheduler candidates.
- VERY_HIGH transactions never release merely because time passed.
- LOW transactions are already `RELEASED` and never enter this scheduler.
- Cancellation is valid only when authoritative time is strictly earlier than `PROTECTED_UNTIL`.
- At the exact deadline, cancellation loses eligibility.
- Every candidate is re-read and revalidated after concurrency control is acquired.
- Scheduler release preserves `RESERVED_AMOUNT`, `RESERVED_AT`, and the active reservation.
- Phase 2.8 will not debit an account, create ledger entries or mark a transaction `SETTLED`; those belong to Phase 2.9.
- Browser countdowns remain display-only.
- Expiry information remains in Oracle, so application restart cannot reset or extend a protection window.
- Duplicate scheduler execution must become a harmless no-op after another worker has won.
- Existing transaction-before-account lock ordering will not be reversed.
- Scheduler activity represents an internal `SYSTEM` actor, never a fabricated customer or administrator.
- Audit/notification/WebSocket expansion will not be prematurely pulled forward from its designated later phase.
